# ONE FLOW: from pressing + to the first file (Columbo)

## Summary

This report covers one moment, about 2 to 60 seconds long. The user
presses **+**, the home turns into fireflies, the fireflies circle the
middle, and then the user gives a file or a folder. The circling
fireflies break loose and become the loose cloud of file fireflies on the
left of the creation screen.

The job people hire this moment for is simple: **"Take my material and
start my agent. Show me that you took it."** They don't come to watch an
animation. They come to hand something over. The fireflies are only
welcome as long as they answer two questions without words: *"where do I
put my stuff?"* and *"did you take it?"*

The main findings:

1. **People read the circle as a "waiting" sign, and that can go two
   ways.** Calm circling around an empty middle with a soft "Select
   Folder" reads as an invitation. But a ring that circles with nothing
   else going on is also what a loading spinner looks like. If the middle
   doesn't clearly invite, people wait for it to "finish" (B3). The
   circle must look ready and still, like it is waiting for *them*, not
   like it is working.
2. **The hand-over is the moment of trust.** When the first file goes
   in, people want proof within about a tenth of a second that it was
   taken: the fireflies react at once. Any gap, any icon, a blank frame,
   or a jump between the orbit and the left side reads as "something
   loaded a new page" or "it broke" (B5, W1, W2).
3. **One file and 10,000 files must feel like the same gesture.** One
   file should not feel thin, and a huge folder should not feel heavy.
   With no counts or status lines on screen (fixes 1 and 7), the
   fireflies are the only sign of progress. They must keep moving
   smoothly while the files are being read (B6, B7).
4. **Many people don't add anything right away.** They press + to look,
   go find the folder in Finder, or get pulled away. The circle has to
   stay calm and patient for minutes. It must never look stuck or demand
   attention, and the way to start must still be clear when they come
   back (B4).

## Scope

**In:**
- pressing + on the home, and the fireflies flying into the orbit around
  the middle (no longer into the button)
- the orbit while it waits: the first seconds, and long waits
- the first file or folder going in, by clicking the middle (Finder
  picker) or by dropping anywhere
- one file versus a big folder (up to 10,000 files), empty folders,
  cancelled pickers, and drops with no files
- the hand-over: the orbit fireflies turning into the left-side cloud,
  and the right-side menu arriving
- Reduce Motion

**Out (and why):**
- **routing files to zones, dim and bright, throwing away:** covered in
  `Reports/creation-screen-behaviours.md` (C2 to C17). This report ends
  when the creation screen is ready.
- **naming the agent, the Sketcher chat:** they come after this moment.
- **adding files to an older agent:** another journey (UJ8).
- **touch and phone:** the app is a desktop window (same assumption as
  before).

## Who is in this moment

- **The clone-maker** (core): has a course folder ready. Wants to see
  it go in.
- **The curious first-timer**: pressed + "to see what happens". Has no
  folder in mind yet.
- **The hurried maker**: already has Finder open, drags before the
  animation ends.
- **The corpus power user**: 5,000 to 10,000 files. Has seen apps freeze
  on big folders.
- **The interrupted user**: presses +, then a call, a message or a
  search for the right folder.
- **The doubter**: was burned before. Watches for any sign of "fake".
- **The Reduce Motion user.**

## Deep dives

### D1 · Press + and find where to put the stuff (B1, B2)

- **The moment:** Evening at the desk, or a quiet block at work. The
  home is open, with one or two agents, or none. They have just decided
  "I'll make one for my course".
- **The trigger:** The + is the only thing that says "new".
- **Thoughts:** "Here we go." Then, while the home dissolves: "Ooh."
  Then, as the fireflies circle: "OK… where do I put the folder? Do I
  click? Do I drop?"
- **Emotions:** Before: a small lift, a decision made. During: delight
  if the motion is quick and smooth, impatience if it is slow. After:
  either "ready" (the middle invites) or "lost" (nothing tells them what
  to do).
- **Expectations:** The fireflies arrive in about a second, as one
  motion, and come to rest circling the middle. The middle is clearly
  *the place*: "Select Folder" shows up there, softly, as soon as the
  ring has formed, not later. They expect both clicking and dropping to
  work, because every upload area they know offers both.
- **Success:** "It's waiting for my folder, right there in the middle."
- **Friction and fears:**
  - The fireflies fly *into the + button* and the screen then rebuilds.
    That feels like two screens with a page load between them (which is
    what it is today). The brief removes this.
  - The ring is there but the middle is empty for a while: "is it still
    loading?" (see D2).
  - Too much motion for too long. Anything over about a second of
    arrival before they can act feels like a cut-scene they can't skip.
- **Product insight:** The + is the start of the same screen, not a
  door to a new one. The fireflies from the home must be the same ones
  that circle, with no blank frame or reload in between. The way in
  ("Select Folder") must be visible the moment the ring stands, and
  clicking and dropping must both work from the first frame. Don't wait
  for the arrival to end (see B8).

### D2 · Waiting: "is it loading?" and the long wait (B3, B4)

- **The moment:** Two kinds.
  - *Short wait* (2 to 10 seconds): the ring is circling, and they hold
    still because they think it is busy.
  - *Long wait* (1 to 30 minutes): they press +, go to Finder to look
    for the right folder, take a call, read a message, and come back.
- **The trigger:** Short: the ring looks like a spinner. Long: life, or
  "where did I put that course?".
- **Thoughts:** Short: "Let it finish loading… Is it done? Is it
  waiting for me?" Long, on coming back: "Where was I? Oh right, the
  folder." Or worse: "Did it time out? Is it still alive?"
- **Emotions:** Short: patient at first, then unsure after about 5
  seconds, then a little silly ("I was waiting for nothing"). Long:
  neutral when they come back, as long as the screen is just as they
  left it.
- **Expectations:**
  - A loading sign *speeds up or changes*. A waiting sign *stays the
    same and stays calm*. They read the ring by its rhythm: slow,
    even and soft means "waiting for you", fast or pulsing means
    "working".
  - After a long time away, the screen is exactly as they left it. No
    "session expired", no reset, no fireflies gone, and nothing that
    made the Mac's fan spin.
- **Success:** Short: they never wait at all, because the middle already
  said "Select Folder". Long: "Still there, still waiting. Drop it in."
- **Friction and fears:**
  - A ring that looks like a spinner. This is the biggest risk of this
    moment: people wait for nothing.
  - "Select Folder" too faint to notice, or showing only on hover. Then
    the only thing on screen is the spinning ring.
  - Something that changes by itself after a while (a hint popping in,
    the ring speeding up, getting brighter). The team rule says no
    dead UI and no nagging. A change people didn't cause reads as "it
    did something", and they wonder what.
  - High CPU or battery use when the window is left open (desktop users
    notice fan noise and a warm laptop).
- **Product insight:** The orbit must look *ready*, not *busy*. It is
  slow, even and dim, and the "Select Folder" invitation sits in the
  middle from the first moment. The speed-up only comes *after* they
  give something (the earlier "fast circling while counting" in C1).
  This also gives the eye a clear before and after: slow means waiting
  for you, a change means "I'm taking it". Over a long wait nothing
  changes by itself. When the window is hidden, the animation pauses
  and picks up where it left off.

### D3 · The first file goes in, and the fireflies break loose (B5, B8)

- **The moment:** They click the middle and pick a folder in Finder, or
  drag a folder from a Finder window onto the app. Sometimes before
  the ring has even settled (B8).
- **The trigger:** They found the folder.
- **Thoughts:** "There." Then, staring: "Did it take it?" When the
  fireflies move: "Oh! They're becoming my files." If anything else
  shows up (an icon, a blank, a jump): "Wait, what was that? Did it
  reload?"
- **Emotions:** Before: a small tension, the hand-over. During: if the
  fireflies answer at once, delight ("it took it"); if they don't,
  doubt. After: a feeling of "it's mine now, it's started", and
  curiosity about the right side.
- **Expectations:**
  - **Under about 0.1 s:** the screen reacts to the drop or the pick.
    This is the limit where people feel "it did it" without thinking
    (Nielsen's response time limits). The reaction is in the fireflies
    themselves: they wake up and start to move.
  - **Within about a second:** the eye can follow *the same* fireflies
    leaving the circle and drifting to the left. If people can't follow
    them, they won't feel the "becoming".
  - **One motion, no stops:** circle, loose, left side, and the menu
    eases in on the right. No step between them they must click
    through.
  - A drop during the arrival (B8) is taken, not ignored. The fireflies
    can change course halfway.
- **Success:** "I gave it my folder and the fireflies turned into my
  files. I watched it happen."
- **Friction and fears:**
  - **An icon or logo in between.** Today the agent's DNA logo shows
    between the orbit and the split. People read an icon as "a
    result", a finish line. It splits one flow into two steps, and they
    wonder if they should click it.
  - **New fireflies instead of the same ones.** If the orbit ones fade
    and new ones appear on the left, the eye loses the thread. The
    doubter reads it as "a fake animation, not my files".
  - **A blink, a jump or a freeze** at the hand-over. Even 200 ms of
    nothing reads as "it hung".
  - **A drop that is silently ignored** because the arrival wasn't done.
    They drop again, and maybe twice.
- **Product insight:** This is the most important second of the flow.
  The hand-over must be one unbroken motion of live fireflies: the same
  objects, with no redraw from scratch, no icon, and no blank frame.
  The first visible reaction comes in the frame after the drop or pick,
  before any files have been read. Drops and picks work at every moment
  of the arrival. Merlin sets the timing; the research limit is
  "reacts at once, readable within a second, no stop in between".

### D4 · One file versus 10,000 files (B6, B7)

- **The moment:**
  - *One file:* a curious first-timer tries their bio PDF, or the
    clone-maker starts with the "core" book.
  - *Big folder:* the clone-maker gives the whole course. 6 GB, 7,000
    files, many videos, `.zip`s and screenshots.
- **The trigger:** "Let's see what it does with this."
- **Thoughts:**
  - One file: "Just one firefly? Is that it?" If it looks right:
    "Cute, that's my bio."
  - Big folder: "That's a lot. Will it choke?" During: "Look at them
    go." If it stalls: "Frozen. Should I force quit?"
- **Emotions:**
  - One file: a small, quiet pleasure if the single firefly feels
    precious. A let-down if 30 fireflies collapse into one dot and the
    screen looks empty and broken.
  - Big folder: worry first, then awe if it stays smooth, then trust.
    One stutter or beach ball and the worry comes back and stays for
    the rest of the session.
- **Expectations:**
  - **Both feel like the same gesture.** The hand-over starts at once
    in both cases. A big folder doesn't make them wait for the reading
    before anything moves.
  - **One file:** the orbit fireflies still break loose in one motion,
    and the left side ends with exactly one file firefly (plus the
    right-side menu). Where the other orbit fireflies go must be calm
    and clear: they fade softly into the dark, they don't vanish in a
    blink. *Assumption:* one file = one firefly on the left, as in the
    earlier report (one firefly per file).
  - **Big folder:** the window keeps responding: the mouse moves, the
    fireflies keep moving, Esc works. The left side keeps filling while
    the files are read, so progress shows without any count (fixes 1
    and 7 removed all counts and status lines). There is a clear end:
    the motion calms down when the reading is done. A few hundred
    fireflies can stand in for 10,000 on screen without anyone
    minding, as long as the cloud keeps growing while it reads and
    then stops.
- **Success:**
  - One file: "One firefly, mine. Clear."
  - Big folder: "It took the whole course without a hiccup."
- **Friction and fears:**
  - A freeze while the folder is being read. This is the single worst
    moment in the flow for the power user (W3 in the earlier report).
  - A very long read with no end in sight. Without counts, the motion
    must show when it's done. Assumption: reading 10,000 file entries
    (names and sizes, not the content) takes a few seconds on a normal
    Mac disk; a slow network drive may take much longer.
  - With one file, the screen looking empty and unfinished.
  - With a huge folder, the left side so crowded it looks like noise or
    static.
- **Product insight:** Start the hand-over at once and let reading run
  behind it, in small batches that never block the screen. The number
  of fireflies on screen may have a cap, but the cloud must grow while
  the reading runs and settle when it ends. That is the only progress
  sign, so it must be honest: it never stops growing while work still
  runs, and never keeps growing after the work is done. One file must
  still look like a deliberate, finished state.

## Watch out (low score, but can break trust)

- **W1 · An icon or logo between the orbit and the chaos.** It reads as
  a finish line or a button, and it breaks the one flow into two. Never.
- **W2 · A blink, reload or jump at the hand-over.** A blank frame, a
  page reload, or fireflies jumping to new places reads as "it broke"
  or "it's fake". The live fireflies move; they are never redrawn from
  scratch.
- **W3 · A freeze with a big folder.** A beach ball or a stuck frame
  while 10,000 files are read loses the power user for good. The screen
  must keep moving, and Esc must work.
- **W4 · A drop that does nothing.** A drop during the arrival, or a
  second drop while the first is read (B13), must not be silently
  lost. Taken, or clearly turned away by the fireflies settling back,
  as today for a drop with no files (`orbit.calm()`).
- **W5 · An empty folder or a cancelled picker.** Must go back to the
  calm waiting ring, as if nothing happened (today's code already does
  this). Never a half-made agent, never an error-coloured flash (team
  rule: no magic on errors).
- **W6 · A ring that looks like a spinner.** People wait for nothing
  (D2). Slow and even before; the change comes only after they give
  something.
- **W7 · The screen changing on its own during a long wait.** No hints
  popping in, no speed-up, no timeout. And the animation pauses when
  the window is hidden, so the laptop stays cool.
- **W8 · Reduce Motion users get a flight.** With Reduce Motion on, the
  ring is still light, and the hand-over is a short cross-fade from the
  orbit to the left-side cloud. No flying, no icon in between.
- **W9 · Esc during the reading leaves half an agent.** Esc while a big
  folder is being read must stop cleanly. *Assumption:* it keeps
  nothing and goes back home, as Esc does today before anything is
  added. Open question Q2.
- **W10 · Private files feel "sent".** The moment of hand-over is when
  the fear peaks ("where did my client calls go?"). Keep it local (it
  is) and add no words or magic about it (team rule).

## What must never happen

Said plainly, for Merlin, Jony and Eraser:

1. **No icon, logo or picture shows between the orbit and the left-side
   chaos.** Not for a frame.
2. **No new screen, page load, blank frame or white flash** between +
   and the creation screen. It is one screen.
3. **The fireflies never fly into the + button.** They fly into the
   orbit around the middle.
4. **The fireflies are never swapped for new ones** at the hand-over.
   The ones you watched are the ones that become your files.
5. **No jump.** No firefly moves from one place to another without the
   eye seeing the path.
6. **No freeze.** Not with one file, not with 10,000. The mouse, the
   fireflies and Esc keep working while the files are read.
7. **The user is never lost about what to do next.** Before: "Select
   Folder" is in the middle from the moment the ring stands. After: the
   left side and the right-side menu are there, and nothing waits for a
   click that isn't asked for.
8. **The waiting ring never looks like a loading spinner,** and never
   changes by itself during a long wait.
9. **No count, no status line, no "loading…" text** appears anywhere in
   this moment (fixes 1 and 7 hold here too).
10. **A drop or pick is never silently lost,** even during the arrival.

## Key insights

1. **The job is "take my material and start my agent, and show me you
   took it."** The fireflies serve the hand-over, not the other way
   round.
2. **Slow means "waiting for you", a change means "I'm taking it".**
   This rhythm is how people tell ready from busy when there are no
   words.
3. **The hand-over is the moment of trust.** React at once, keep one
   unbroken motion, same fireflies, no icon.
4. **One file and 10,000 files are the same gesture.** Both start
   moving at once; the big folder simply keeps the cloud growing until
   it is done.
5. **The cloud is the only progress sign,** because counts and status
   lines are gone. So it must be honest: growing while reading,
   settling when done.
6. **Waiting is normal.** The ring must be patient, unchanged and cheap
   to run for as long as it takes.

## Open questions (with the assumption we work with until answered)

- **Q1 · Where do the extra orbit fireflies go when only one file comes
  in?** *Assumption:* about 30 orbit fireflies break loose, one becomes
  the file firefly and the rest fade softly into the dark as they
  drift. Merlin decides the motion.
- **Q2 · Esc during the reading of a big folder.** *Assumption:* it stops
  the reading, keeps nothing and goes home, like Esc before any file.
  The earlier report (C11) asked for the same clean stop.
- **Q3 · A second folder dropped while the first is still read (B13).**
  *Assumption:* it is added to the same agent once the creation screen
  owns dropping, as the creation screen already does today. During the
  hand-over itself, it waits its turn rather than being lost.
- **Q4 · How many fireflies stand in for 10,000 files.** *Assumption:* a
  few hundred, chosen by Merlin and Jony. The research need is only
  that the cloud grows while reading and stops when done.
- **Q5 · What starts the hand-over: the first file found, or the pick
  itself?** *Assumption:* the pick or drop itself. People need the
  reaction within 0.1 s, before even the first file entry has been read.
