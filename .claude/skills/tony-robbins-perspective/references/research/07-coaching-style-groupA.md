# 07 — Coaching Style (Group A)

This is literally Robbins coaching on camera — the second-richest dimension in this batch alongside
Conversations. Evidence given per sub-dimension with quote + source file. Where evidence is genuinely absent,
marked "insufficient evidence" rather than invented.

## 1. Question ratio (clarifying/challenging questions before advice)

Ratio is consistently high and front-loaded: long stretches of pure questioning before any directive
statement. In **Jessica**, the opening ~10 exchanges are entirely questions ("Are you feeling depressed right
now? ... At this moment? ... What is your name ma'am? Where are you from?") before the first interpretive
statement appears. In **Carol1**, Robbins asks roughly a dozen questions ("How do you know?... How come?...
Where do your spiritual beliefs go?") before his first piece of direct guidance. In **OleSuzanne**, the
entire first third of the conference demo is Cloé and Tony alternating clarifying questions about the money
dynamic before any reframe is offered. Advice/directive statements, when they come, are usually short and
immediately followed by a *new* question testing whether it landed ("So the question is this: Does this make
sense to you?" — Jessica). Across every file in this batch, questions substantially outnumber declarative
coaching statements in the diagnostic phase of a session.

## 2. Challenge vs. support balance, and what triggers escalation to more direct/confrontational mode

Support (applause cues, "I honor you," naming courage) is the default register when a client discloses
something painful or admits a flaw: "Give her a hand for that level of honesty. That is amazing" (SamDaryl);
"I really honor you for that level of honesty" (Tahnee); "Give him a hand for that... That's fantastic"
(DanaGreg, re: Greg admitting his pattern).

Escalation to confrontation reliably triggers on one specific thing: **a client generalizing in absolutist
terms, or using a story to avoid responsibility/action** — not on emotional intensity itself. Examples:
"That's not true. Don't lie to me" (Carol1, triggered by "every time I think about it"); "little bastards...
why are you liking me calling him something because you will not say it" (Jessica, triggered by Jessica
protecting her husband from blunt language while still blaming him); "All it really is is what? ...
Bullshit" (Giulia, triggered by Giulia's self-pity narrative). The common thread: he tolerates raw emotion
(crying, anger, despair) without confrontation, but escalates sharply the instant he judges a client is
*using* a story to stay stuck or avoid a concrete commitment.

## 3. Concretization — forcing vague statements into specific, measurable commitments

The dominant concretizing tool across the whole batch is the **zero-to-ten need scale**, applied to both
client and (hypothetically) their partner: "How certain are you he loves you? ... How much variety?"
(Jessica, applied to Jessica's and Orlando's needs); full before/after scaling of all six needs in
**OleSuzanne** (certainty 4→10, variety 5→8, significance 7→10, etc.) explicitly used to measure the session's
effect in real time.

Beyond scaling, he forces vagueness into *behavior*: "What would you do? Not just say, but do?" (SamDaryl);
"How will you be there? When it's easy? ... How would you be there when it is easy, when it is hard all the
time? How?" (SamDaryl, pressing Daryl past the vague "I'll be there" into specifics). He also concretizes
identity itself by making clients name a part of themselves (Molly's "Cinnamon," Olivia's "Garzon") so a vague
feeling becomes an invokable, repeatable handle.

## 4. Resistance handling — deflection, defensiveness, disagreement

When a client deflects or hedges, Robbins typically **restates the question and refuses to accept a non-
answer**: "Is it a 'yes' or is it a 'no'?" (Carol1, after Carol says "I guess"); when Ocean (Ocean1) deflects
a direct question about self-harm with a non-answer and a long pause, he stops the interrogation entirely and
asks for trust explicitly before repeating the question ("Do you trust me? ... to love you and not to let you
be hurt?").

When a client disagrees or pushes back mid-exercise, he does not retreat — he holds the frame and sometimes
uses humor to defuse the friction without abandoning the point: when Sam accuses him of colluding with Daryl
("You are just agreeing with him"), he agrees sarcastically ("Oh, yeah, they are just agreeing with me") and
continues the exercise unchanged (SamDaryl). When Giulia defends her mother against Tony's own deliberately
provocative language ("Your mother, that whore" / "That is my mother, not yours"), he immediately validates
the boundary ("I'm glad we have made that separation clear") rather than pushing past it — showing resistance
handling is not uniformly confrontational; he distinguishes defensiveness-to-be-broken from a legitimate
boundary-to-be-respected.

## 5. Reframing moves — breaking false binaries / shifting the frame

Recurring signature reframe: **recasting a "problem" behavior as a request for love/attention rather than an
attack**. "What she is really calling for?... How does she get your attention when she really wants it?"
(OleSuzanne, reframing Suzanne's overspending); "getting depressed is one way that she can get the love of
the people around her" (Jessica). A second recurring reframe: **naming a false binary and collapsing it** —
"Is no movement presence? Is he supposed to stare and not have any movement or is he supposed to be feeling
and present with you?" (SamDaryl); "You can be right or you can be in love" (SamDaryl). A third: **re-typing
an emotion as energy to be used rather than fought** — the aikido/fear-wrist-grab metaphor (Marriage2); Sam's
rage reframed as untapped "fire" (SamDaryl) versus elsewhere reframing the *same kind* of anger as pure
pathology to eliminate (Giulia, Tahnee) — see the contradiction flagged in 01-writings.

## 6. Pacing & arc — how a session typically opens, builds, peaks, resolves

Consistent shape across the stand-up-from-the-audience interventions (Jessica, SandraSandra, Tahnee, Jim1,
Carol1, SevenMasterSteps/Gary+Ken+Olivia, Giulia): (1) **open** with an audience-wide call for hands
("Raise your hand if you are suicidal/depressed") and select one person; (2) **rapid diagnostic
questioning** narrowing from the stated surface problem to the underlying need/pattern, usually within a few
minutes; (3) a **named-pattern or named-identity moment** (the "Crazy Eight," "Garzon," "Cinnamon," the
chair demonstration) that crystallizes the diagnosis; (4) a **peak-intensity moment** — a visualization, a
scripted ritual, a physical exercise, or a provocative confrontation — that produces visible emotional
release (tears, laughter, or both); (5) **resolution via an explicit declaration or commitment** stated in the
client's own words; (6) **audience applause** closing the beat. The two-person conference-demo format
(OleSuzanne) follows a flatter but structurally similar arc without the stand-up-from-audience opening.
Several films (SamDaryl, Jessica, Carol1, OleSuzanne, Jim1) append a **follow-up interview months or years
later**, a production convention showing durability-of-change claims, not necessarily part of the live
coaching technique itself.

## 7. Closing mechanism — what he insists on before ending

He consistently insists on one of: (a) a **first-person declaration spoken aloud** ("I, Giulia, see, hear,
feel and know that I will never again indulge in..." — Giulia); (b) a **direct promise to the specific person
involved**, not to Robbins ("I will show you the love you've never seen before" — SamDaryl, Daryl to Sam;
"Chelsea, my new rule is to live life with love" — SevenMasterSteps/Gary, spoken to his deceased daughter,
not to Tony); (c) an explicit **homework assignment with a deadline or measurable structure** — DanaGreg
closes with a numbered four-step take-home list; Giulia closes with a specific daily practice ("repeat this
statement... at least 50 times") and a request to "get supporters" and write letters; Jessica's film closes
with an explicit "90-day challenge" instruction to the viewer. He rarely ends a segment on an open question —
the close is reliably a stated commitment, not an open loop.

## 8. Presence cues — tone/vocal/physical, only where the transcript itself notes them

The transcripts do include bracketed stage directions and narrator notes; these are reported here verbatim,
not invented:
- **[Laughs] / [Applause] / [audience laughing]** — pervasive throughout nearly every file.
- **"Tony is now crying."** (Carol1, CM narration, during the role-reversal exercise where he plays "Carol.")
- **A long, explicitly noted pause before a disclosure**: "[Long pause]" immediately before Ocean answers the
  question about her legs (Ocean1).
- **Noted physical details during an emotional peak**: "Why are his eyes watering? He's still present though
  even though his eyes are watering" (SamDaryl, re: Daryl); "[Sam laughs]" and "[doubtfully]" tags on specific
  lines (SamDaryl); "[shaking her head]," "[almost crying]" (SamDaryl, re: Sam).
- **Vocal/physical description used diagnostically, not just as color**: in Tahnee, Robbins himself narrates
  Tahnee's physiology shift in real time ("she's got one arm here, but the other one is loose... her head is
  kind of bouncing like this. That's called hopeful for her") — here the presence cue is not a stage
  direction but part of his own spoken commentary, a notable case where "presence cue" and "coaching content"
  merge.

No stage directions describing his own posture, breathing, or physical staging beyond what's quoted above
were found in this batch; **insufficient evidence** for anything more granular (e.g. described vocal pitch,
pacing of his own speech) beyond what is explicitly bracketed in the transcripts.
