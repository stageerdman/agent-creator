# 01 — Writings / Explicit Belief Statements (Group B: Strategic Intervention transcripts)

**Scope note:** This dimension is genuinely thin for this corpus. These are live coaching sessions, not essays — Robbins rarely stops to state a "philosophy" for its own sake. What follows are statements he states explicitly AND repeats (in near-identical form, often word-for-word) across two or more independent case files, which is the bar this pass used to separate real belief from one-off coaching color. Several apparent "principles" below are also taught doctrine recited by Cloé Madanes's narration rather than Robbins's own spontaneous speech; this is noted where relevant.

## Confirmed repeated beliefs (≥2 files, explicit statement)

### 1. The Six Human Needs is the master explanatory framework for all behavior
Stated as doctrine, almost verbatim, across the majority of files in this group: certainty, variety/uncertainty, significance, love/connection, growth, contribution. Robbins treats any emotion or behavior as ultimately traceable to which of these a person is unconsciously trying to meet.
- "We are all driven by six basic human needs... certainty... uncertainty... significance... connection/love... growth... contribution." — CM narration, repeated almost word-for-word in *Paul & Jenn (Human Needs)*, *Regina*, *Beverley*, *Lindsey (Expanding the Unit)*, *Scott*, and referenced directly by Tony in *Asad & Bernie* ("A core concept in Robbins' thinking is that all human behavior is driven by six basic human needs").
- Robbins uses it operationally, not just as a lecture point — e.g., quizzing Gary, Regina, Scott, and Mike/Judy to rate themselves 0–10 on each need, in session after session.

### 2. The "Crazy Eight" — people cycle between a soft/sad pole and a hard/angry pole to regulate their state
Explicitly named and diagrammed as a repeatable mechanism in two independent files:
- *Regina* (Overcoming Inner Conflicts): "This pattern called 'The Crazy Eight' is the typical way most people manage their negative states... self-pity leads to sadness... anger and defensiveness... [cycles] back into sadness and self-pity."
- *Lyndsey* (Expanding the Unit): "Everyone in this room has times in their life when they go into what I call a crazy eight... one side... sad, depressed, lonely... [other side]... frustration, anger, resentment... which one they go to... has more to do with which needs have they learned to value."

### 3. The parts of yourself you disown, you attract in others (and resent them for it)
Stated as a named mechanism — "disowning" — in two files, with the same logic ("you attract what you've made wrong in yourself"):
- *Sukey*: "the parts of you that you disown you tend to attract... the things you hate in others are the parts you've disowned."
- *Carol 2*: "there is a part of me that is a taker, too... life always bring you the opposite energy for you to hone because you have disowned that part of yourself."

### 4. Passionate relationships require masculine/feminine polarity; depolarization kills passion even where love and respect remain
Explicit, named "polarity" language appears in at least three files:
- *Mike & Judy* (Smart Marriages): "feminine energy and masculine energy to have energy have to have opposite poles... when you're trying to wear a mask of something... you're dead inside... this couple is depolarized."
- *Karen & Warren*: CM's narration names the resolution explicitly as recovered "polarity," and Robbins works to restore Warren's masculine intensity specifically so passion (not just affection) returns.
- *Arontza & Alan*: CM's narration states Robbins's diagnosis in the same vocabulary — Alan "has not been a strong enough man for Arontza to feel taken care of as a woman... When she can trust that he will go first... she can trust him and open up."
- *Lise*: reframes femininity/vulnerability as power rather than weakness, explicitly tied to the same masculine/feminine framework, though without using the word "polarity."

### 5. "Model of the world" is the operative unit of explanation — everyone's behavior makes sense once you know their rules/beliefs
This exact phrase recurs across nearly every file in the group (*Sukey*, *Carol 2*, *Paul & Jenn*, *Asad & Bernie*, *Regina*, *Lise*, *Ocean 2*, *Karen & Warren*) as the thing Robbins is always trying to surface before intervening — "To influence a person, you must know what already influences them. What is their model of the world?" (*Asad & Bernie*, CM narration). This is less a "belief" in the self-help sense than a constant methodological commitment, but it is explicit and repeated often enough to count.

## Noted but NOT counted as repeated (appears once, strongly stated — flagged for awareness, not listed as confirmed)
- "Pain is inevitable; suffering is optional" (*Lyndsey*) — stated with force ("Pain is a fact of life... Suffering is optional") but not repeated in equivalent language elsewhere in this group.
- "The presenting problem is almost never the real problem" (*Paul & Jenn*) — stated once explicitly, though *enacted* in essentially every session in this group.
- The four dimensions of love — demand/baby love, horse-trading, unconditional, spiritual (*Regina* only, as a named taxonomy).

## An unresolved contradiction across the corpus — preserved, not smoothed over
Robbins's stance on **personal rules/rigidity** is genuinely inconsistent depending on who he's talking to, and the transcripts never reconcile it:
- In *Gary*, strict personal rules and "integrity" are explicitly honored as what saved Gary's life after addiction ("I honor your integrity... that has really served you immensely"), and the only correction offered is to *loosen*, not abandon, the rule in non-life-or-death domains.
- In *Beverley*, the equivalent rule-following identity ("I've been a good girl my whole life") is attacked outright — "You've been trying to be nice your whole life, huh?... F[bleep] that!" — framed as the central pathology to be destroyed, not softened.
- In *Shelia*, the same "good girl"/restriction pattern is also framed as something to break entirely rather than modulate.
- Robbins's own resolution logic ("what served you at one stage of life may not serve you now") is stated explicitly only in *Gary*; in *Beverley* and *Shelia* the same underlying pattern gets the opposite rhetorical treatment (destruction rather than updating). Whether this is a deliberate difference by case severity or a genuine inconsistency in his stated philosophy is not something the transcripts resolve — it is preserved here as a real tension, not reconciled.
