# 03 — Expression DNA: Recurring Phrasing, Rhetorical Tics, Tone (Group B: Strategic Intervention transcripts)

## Catchphrases / stock constructions that recur across many files

- **"Give her/him a hand, ladies and gentlemen"** — used after nearly every vulnerable disclosure, dozens of times across almost every file (*Carol 2*, *Sukey*, *Paul & Jenn*, *Lindsey*, *Beverley*, *Scott*, *Gary*, *Mike & Judy*, *Arontza & Alan*). Functions as both audience cue and a way of publicly rewarding honesty the instant it appears.
- **"That's it. That's it."** (or "That's right. That's right.") — rapid-fire verbal affirmation repeated multiple times in a row at the exact moment someone is mid-breakthrough, especially during the archetype work (*Sukey*, *Shelia*) and the physical/embodied exercises (*Lise*'s breast-touch sequence, *Maggie*'s breathing exercise).
- **Zero-to-ten / zero-to-a-hundred scaling** — his single most consistent linguistic tic for turning a feeling into something workable: "On a zero to ten, how certain are you...?" appears constantly across *Paul & Jenn*, *Regina*, *Scott*, *Mike & Judy*, *Beverley*, *Gary*. He uses it on strangers within the first few exchanges of meeting them.
- **"How many of you..." / "Say 'I'"** — direct audience call-and-response, used to both teach the framework to the room and to normalize the speaker's experience ("How many of you have ever had somebody..." in *Sukey*, *Lindsey*, *Mike & Judy*).
- **"Give me a hand for [X]"** immediately followed by naming exactly what is being honored — not generic praise but specific ("Give her a hand for that level of honesty," *Arontza & Alan*; "Give her a hand for the honesty," *Mike & Judy*).
- **"Honey" / "sweetheart" / "my dear"** as address, overwhelmingly directed at women in distress (*Lise*, *Carol 2*, *Paul & Jenn* with Jen, *Karen & Warren*), functioning as a softening device that often immediately precedes or follows a harsh confrontation.
- **Terms of endearment paired with profanity in the same breath** — a specific and distinctive combination, e.g., "you crazy [bleep]" said with affection (*Mike & Judy*: "I love you, you crazy ____"), "you devils" (*Arontza & Alan*, *Mike & Judy*), "you blue breaking bastard, you" (*Gary*, delivered with a laugh).
- **"I see."** — a short, flat acknowledgment he drops in constantly, often right before pivoting the conversation somewhere the person didn't expect (appears dozens of times across *Karen & Warren*, *Lise*, *Scott*, *Paul & Jenn*).
- **"Give me the [X]" as a structured retrieval device** — e.g., "Give me the pinky one. The time you felt totally loved at a ten by him" (*Mike & Judy*), mapping memories onto fingers one at a time — a distinctive mnemonic/rhetorical device, not just a question.

## Rhetorical tics

- **Escalating "what else?" chains** until the person runs dry of pat answers (*Sukey*, *Carol 2*, *Beverley* — "What else? What else?").
- **Answering his own question for the person by voicing what he suspects, then checking it** — "I know you want to say [X]. But what's the real number?" (*Mike & Judy*, to Michael, re: self-rated significance).
- **Repeating the client's own word back as a question**, often just a single word or short phrase, functioning as a verbal magnifying glass: "Confused?" / "She's confused, ladies and gentlemen!" (*Arontza & Alan*); "Pretending?" (*Shelia*).
- **"The [archetype/part] says..." sentence-starter drilling** — a structured incantation he has the client repeat and complete themselves, used identically in three separate parts-work sequences (*Sukey*'s sisters, *Asad & Bernie*'s four archetypes, *Shelia*'s four archetypes).
- **Flat, declarative one-liners dropped after a long emotional build**, often the entire "insight" compressed to under ten words: "Vulnerability is power. It's not a weakness." (*Lise*); "Pain is a damn good thing if you use it." (*Lindsey*); "You can't come to a positive side of this warrior." (*Asad & Bernie*).

## Humor style

- **Playful insult as rapport**, almost always immediately defused or honored right after: calling a suicidal woman "a wuss" for her choice of pills (*Karen & Warren*), then immediately moving to serious diagnostic questions; teasing Gary about wanting a BMW ("you blue breaking bastard, you") right after praising his integrity.
- **Comic absurdism used to interrupt grief or self-pity** rather than straight reassurance — the sperm-race monologue (*Maggie*); "Now, let's talk about getting laid... 'cause that's the real reason you're depressed" (*Maggie*, immediately after a tearful breakthrough, to deliberately shift register).
- **Mock-scolding/faux outrage as a form of praise** — "Boy, you're in deep sh[bleep]... Been getting cuddles on the side" (*Arontza & Alan*, teasing Arontza about enjoying hugs from others more than from her husband).
- **Self-aware jokes about his own role/fame** — "I know everyone will think you're psychotic and all of them will know that you had your brain washed by me" (*Beverley*); referring to himself in the third person as "that Robbins guy" voiced through a skeptical imagined husband (*Lindsey*).

## Confidence / certainty markers in how he talks

- **Appeals to raw sample size as evidence of authority**: "I've been with three million people" / "I've dealt with people from 80 countries" — repeated verbatim or near-verbatim across *Carol 2*, *Asad & Bernie*, *Karen & Warren*, *Lindsey* — functioning as his standard warrant for a generalization about human nature.
- **Flat refusal to hedge on diagnosis once he's made it**: "I know." / "I know that's true." (repeated short assertions, *Carol 2*, *Lise*) rather than "I think" or "maybe."
- **Explicit acknowledgment of his own uncertainty paired immediately with confident redirection** — a notable and consistent pattern: "I don't know the answer. I'm not the guy who's wise about this. You are." (*Asad & Bernie*) — he is willing to disclaim expertise about content while remaining completely certain about process (what question to ask next).

## Distinctive word choices

- **"Model of the world"** — used as a technical term, not just colloquially, dozens of times.
- **"Significant" / "significance"** as a near-constant diagnostic noun, used far more often than synonyms like "important" or "valued."
- **"Honey," "sweetheart"** skew almost entirely toward female clients; male clients get "sir," "my friend," or direct name use more often — a gendered asymmetry worth noting as a pattern, not a one-off.
- **Clinical/technical vocabulary dropped into otherwise plain or crude speech** — "scotoma" (*Scott*), "double bind" (*Karen & Warren*), "triangulation" / "cross generation coalition" (*Ocean 2*, via CM narration but echoed by Tony's own usage) — creates a register shift from street-level bluntness to clinical authority within the same conversation.

- Specific physical stage directions preserved in the *Arontza & Alan* transcript (unusually detailed compared to other files): `{Alan pushes his hands downward to get centered}`, `{Alan kisses Arontza and looks in her eyes}`, `{Arontza finally shifts and holds Alan, her head on his shoulder}`.
- `[Beverly throws off her sweater, audience cheers]` and `[Tony kisses Beverly on the cheek]` (*Beverley*) — physical escalation noted directly in-text.
- `[W crushes chair]` (*Karen & Warren*) — the single most dramatic physical stage direction in the group.
- Vocal/emotional markers transcribed directly: `[crying]`, `[Sukey laughs, audience cheers]`, `[whispered]`, `[Tony inhales rapidly]` (the breathing exercise in *Maggie* has its own repeated transcribed breath-sound notation).
