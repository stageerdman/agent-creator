## Belief 1: The Six Human Needs is the master explanatory framework for all behavior
Stated almost identically, as formal doctrine (not improvised), in at least seven of the sixteen files:
SamDaryl, Marriage1, Marriage2, Jessica, SevenMasterSteps, Giulia, OleSuzanne, Tahnee, and referenced in
Sandra and Carol1. Core formula, repeated nearly word-for-word:

> "There are six human needs... Certainty, Variety [uncertainty], Significance, Connection and Love, Growth,
> and Contribution... The first four are the needs of the personality... everybody meets them even if you
> got to lie to yourself. The final two... are the needs of the spirit." (Tahnee; near-identical wording in
> Marriage1/2, SevenMasterSteps, OleSuzanne)

This is the single most repeated explicit belief in the corpus — a genuine distinctive doctrine (named,
numbered, taught the same way every time), not a generic self-help truism.

## Belief 2: Love is not a trade — giving-to-get is "whoring," not love
Stated as explicit moral vocabulary (he flags the word choice as deliberate) in Marriage2 ("I use that word
very deliberately to jar you... what does a whore do? They only love you as long as you pay them"),
SevenMasterSteps/Michelle ("which level is she playing... level two love always makes you feel like you are
a whore"), and Jessica ("another word for horse trading is... whoring"). The four-level typology of love
(baby/demand love → horse-trading/whoring → giving because it's who you are → loving even those who hurt
you, citing the Dalai Lama/Gandhi) is repeated near-verbatim in Marriage2 and Jessica, and the first principle
("put your lover first... if you really love somebody, what will you do for them? Anything") recurs in
Marriage1, Marriage2, SamDaryl, and OleSuzanne.

## Belief 3: The two universal human fears — "I'm not enough" and "I won't be loved"
Stated as an explicit, named pair of universal fears in Marriage2 ("The two fears all human beings have... I
can tell you beyond the shadow of doubt... not enough... and if I'm not enough then I won't be loved") and
again in Tahnee using identical language ("the primary fear that we won't... are not enough and won't be
loved"). Presented both times as settled fact ("beyond a shadow of doubt," "it's never going to go away").

## Belief 4: Emotion is produced by three controllable levers — physiology, focus, language
Stated as formal doctrine ("Principle of the Triad") in Tahnee, and the same three-part model (with the same
90s-pop-psych statistic — "words are 7%... physiology is 55%... voice is 38%") appears in Marriage2. The
same structure, without the statistic, underlies Jim1 ("these are the three decisions that shape your life:
What are you going to focus on? What does it mean? What are you going to do?") and Carol1 (CM narration
paraphrases the identical physiology/focus/language model). Repetition of the specific percentages across
two films, plus the model's reuse (without stats) in two more, makes this a confirmed standing belief rather
than an improvised analogy.

## Belief 5: "The only pain people ever experience is from holding back love"
Stated nearly verbatim in Marriage2 ("The only pain people ever experience is from holding back their love.
That's where it always comes from") and again to Michelle in SevenMasterSteps ("when you withhold your love,
all pain you will ever experience in your life is because you withheld your love from anyone"). Treated as
an absolute law each time, not a situational observation.

## Belief 6: All human behavior has a positive intent
Stated explicitly as a creed in DanaGreg ("I believe with my soul that all human behavior has a positive
intent... It may have a negative impact, but the initial intent is positive"). This is the only file where it
is stated this explicitly as a named principle, but it is the operating assumption visible in practice across
Jessica, Giulia, Tahnee, and SevenMasterSteps/Gary (every "symptom" — depression, self-pity, overeating,
strictness — is traced back to a need the behavior is serving). Flagged as a genuinely distinctive,
repeatedly-applied belief even though only one file states it as a formal maxim.

## Contradiction to preserve — "love no matter what" vs. a stated limit on it
Robbins repeatedly states the absolute-sounding rule "love them no matter what" (Marriage2, applied to
Jessica and to Daryl in SamDaryl), but in the same Marriage2 lecture he explicitly caveats it: "I'm not saying
stay with them if they're going to be abusive... The Dalai Lama isn't sitting there letting people try to
kill him, he left, but he still has love." This is a real tension in the material — "no matter what" is
stated as if unconditional, then immediately qualified — and should not be smoothed into a single consistent
rule.

## Contradiction to preserve — anger/intensity as resource vs. anger/intensity as "crazy-eight" pathology to eliminate
In SamDaryl, Sam's rage is explicitly reframed as attractive, valuable energy ("I love her willfulness...
imagine what you can unleash in this woman... This is a worthy woman"). In Tahnee and Giulia, a near-identical
anger pattern ("pissed off," used to escape depression) is instead named as a destructive, addictive "crazy
eight" to be eradicated ("that's bullshit... it's a joke... it's destructive"). The valence Robbins assigns to
the same emotional pattern (anger as a defense against vulnerability) differs by case — sometimes it is
honored as untapped fire, sometimes it is targeted for elimination. Preserved here as a genuine inconsistency,
not resolved.

## Assessment
Evidence volume for this dimension: **moderate**, concentrated in a small number of recurring, named
doctrines (Six Human Needs; four levels of love; two fears; physiology/focus/language triad; "love is not a
trade"; positive intent). Outside of these repeated formal structures, most of what Robbins says in-session is
improvised application, not standalone belief statement — consistent with the expectation that this dimension
would be thin in a transcript corpus. No padding with generic self-help truisms has been added; items above
were kept only where they repeat, in similar language, across independent files.
