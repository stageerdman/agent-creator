# 02 — Conversations: Live Dialogue Technique (Group A)

This is the richest dimension in this corpus — these are transcripts of Robbins doing the thing live, one on
one, in front of an audience. Evidence below is organized by technique, each with source-file citations.
Duplicates (Bonus_Smart_Marriages_Part_1 = Marriage1; Bonus_Smart_Marriages_Part_2 = Marriage2) are cited
once under the primary filename. Jim_2 is the broken 404 stub and contributes nothing here.

## Question patterns

**Opening diagnostic triad, asked almost verbatim across cases**: "What are you focusing on? What does it
mean? What are you going to do?" — stated as a named three-decision model in Jim1 ("these are the three
decisions that shape your life"), and functionally reused as the opening interrogation shape in Jessica,
Carol1, and SevenMasterSteps. He rarely opens with advice; he opens by locating focus, meaning, and intended
action.

**Zero-to-ten scaling as a near-universal probe.** Used to force an emotional claim into a number before he
will act on it: "How depressed are you... Ten is totally depressed... zero is not depressed at all" (Jessica,
Tahnee); "How certain are you that you can take your relationship to a deeper place..." (OleSuzanne, scaling
all six needs before and after the intervention); "How much connection... zero to ten?" (Giulia, scaling the
Crazy Eight). This is close to a signature move — question ratio is high specifically because almost every
emotional claim gets re-asked as a 0–10 number before he proceeds.

**Forced binary / "yes or no" close-outs** that don't let a vague answer stand: "Do you believe he loves you?
Yes or no?" (Jessica); "Is that a 'yes' or is it a 'no'?" (Carol1, refusing Carol's hedged "I guess the answer
is no"); "Are you willing to let this emotion live in your life? Yes or no?" (Giulia).

## Improvised analogies and metaphors (a different one almost every file — this is where he is most clearly
improvising rather than running a script)

- **"Try to pick up the chair"** (SamDaryl): a live physical demonstration that "trying" is a nervous-system
  alibi, not an action — built on the spot as a direct analogue for Sam's "I tried for six weeks."
- **Aikido wrist-grab** (Marriage2/Bonus Part 2): a volunteer grabs his wrist; he shows that fighting fear
  tightens its grip, but "going with it" and agreeing with the fear defuses it — explicit metaphor for how to
  "dance with" the fear of not being enough.
- **The missing salt / "scotoma"** (Marriage2): the childhood story of being told to find salt that's right in
  front of you and genuinely not seeing it — used to explain how couples "hypnotize themselves" into not
  perceiving what's there.
- **Burping lessons** (Jim1): when Jim can't access emotion verbally, Robbins has another audience member
  physically teach Jim to burp on command — an improvised, almost absurd physical metaphor for releasing
  what's stuck in the throat, which Robbins then explicitly narrates back to the grief/identity theme.
- **The "Garzon" family name** (SevenMasterSteps/Olivia): discovering Olivia's maiden name mid-session and
  building an entire identity-based intervention around it ("As a Garzon, would you sit there and cry?") —
  invented in real time from one incidental fact.
- **"Cinnamon"** (SevenMasterSteps/Michelle): asking Michelle to name her sexual/playful self something other
  than her own name, landing on "Cinnamon," then using that name as a reusable handle for the rest of the
  intervention and the take-home instruction to her husband ("Your job is to constantly love Mom and take
  Cinnamon").
- **Akido-adjacent reframes of resistance as a "game"**: "This is not a problem. This is a game you don't
  know you're in" (OleSuzanne) — reframing Suzanne's overspending as a game for attention rather than a
  conflict to be solved.

## Visible mid-session tack changes (explicitly narrated, not inferred)

- **SamDaryl**: Cloé Madanes's narration explicitly flags a changed plan: "Tony had intended to take Samantha
  and Daryl to stage three: sharing, listening and learning... However, the level of trust in this couple is
  so low that Samantha is afraid to communicate honestly. Tony will now take Daryl back to stage one." This
  is a rare instance of the methodology's own narration documenting Robbins abandoning his plan mid-session
  because the couple wasn't ready.
- **SevenMasterSteps/Gary**: narration notes he "skips to step three" out of the normal seven-step order when
  a faster pattern-interrupt is available, then returns to the fuller sequence once Gary is destabilized
  enough to go deeper.
- **Jessica**: starts by asking her to get *more* depressed on purpose (intensifying before interrupting),
  switches instantly to a fart joke the moment she hits peak depression, then pivots entirely to a role-play
  where Jessica plays her husband "Orlando" — three different modes inside a few minutes.

## Refusing/calling out dishonesty and generalization — a recurring "I won't accept that" move

Robbins repeatedly refuses to accept absolutist or self-protective language and names it directly as lying:
"Don't lie to me, because if I'm going to help you, you're not going to lie to me" (Carol1, confronting "every
time I think about it"); "That's not true... You snapped at him not because you don't love him" (SamDaryl);
calling Jessica's "I did everything" a pattern he won't let stand ("Bull, if you gave them everything, they
would want to be with you" — stated generically in Marriage2 and applied directly to Jessica).

## Working the client's own words back at them

Instead of correcting content, he frequently repeats the client's literal phrase back as a question, forcing
them to hear it: "You said I have never given my heart" (SamDaryl, Daryl/Sam exchange); echoing Michelle's "I
want to be the wife he wants me to be" back to the room as the diagnosis itself (SevenMasterSteps).

## Role-play / stepping into another person's body — a repeated structural device

Used in at least four files to build empathy or access a stuck emotional position: Jessica is asked to speak
*as* her husband Orlando, answering as him; Gary (SevenMasterSteps) is guided into an imagined conversation
with his dead daughter Chelsea, speaking for her; Jim1 and Carol1's coach/client roles are *swapped* —
Robbins has Carol play "Tony" and coach him while he plays a despairing "Carol," explicitly as a device to get
Carol to hear her own advice from outside herself.

## Dual-channel address — talking to the individual while narrating generalizations to the room

A consistent structural habit: he answers the person in front of him but periodically turns outward to make
the moment a lesson for the whole audience ("Ladies, if you really want to understand a man... hear what this
man just told you" — Jim1; "guys, could you imagine me saying men need attention all the time and you
cheering?" — SevenMasterSteps/Michelle). This happens in nearly every seminar-floor intervention (not the
two-person-only OleSuzanne conference demo, which has no live audience vote-of-hands moments, though it still
occasionally addresses "the room" of therapists).

## What he refuses to engage with

- **Refuses to let a client leave the conversation about to self-harm unresolved**: in Jim1, when Jim
  explicitly withdraws consent ("I don't give you permission to do that"), Robbins does not negotiate — he
  overrides it ("I don't really think you have any authority over me") while naming the cost ("I know I've
  lost all your trust right now but..."). This is the clearest instance in the corpus of him refusing a
  client's stated boundary, justified by a stated duty of care.
- **Refuses analytical/intellectual escape routes.** When Ole in OleSuzanne reflects appreciatively ("I really
  love this experience... this is a great exercise"), Robbins corrects him instantly: "It's your life, it's
  not an exercise any more" — refusing to let the client retreat into observer language.
- **Refuses to discuss the client's past trauma as the "real" issue when he judges it's a decoy.** In SamDaryl
  he deliberately does not dwell on Sam's disclosed childhood rape once he has it ("Tony decided right then to
  respect Sam's wish not to focus on her past and to focus instead on rebuilding her relationship skills");
  in SamDaryl's own follow-up interview Sam states this herself: "that was not the deepest issue that I had...
  It was an excuse."

## Working a client's emotional state — pattern-interrupt as the central live tool

The single most repeated live technique across every file in this batch is some form of **deliberate state
interruption**: intensify the unwanted state on command, then break it instantly with humor, a surprising
question, or a physical cue (farting jokes in Jessica and SevenMasterSteps/Olivia; "did you check out that
last move" in Tahnee; the chime/music cue noted in SevenMasterSteps/Gary). The explicit theory stated across
files is that this proves the emotion is chosen/produced, not simply happening to the person — he states this
rationale nearly identically in Tahnee and Jessica.
