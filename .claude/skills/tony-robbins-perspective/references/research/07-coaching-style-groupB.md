# 07 — Coaching Style (Group B: Strategic Intervention transcripts)

This is the richest dimension in the corpus alongside Conversations (02) — these transcripts are literally Robbins coaching on camera. Each sub-dimension below gives concrete transcript evidence with source citations; nothing is invented.

## 1. Question ratio (clarifying/challenging questions before advice)

Overwhelmingly question-first. In most files the ratio of questions-to-statements before the first real directive is roughly 10:1 or higher.
- *Sukey*: ~40 consecutive Q&A exchanges (father's rules → what she "had to be" → what she "couldn't be") before the first reframe ("The challenge is every one of us in this room is Sukey...").
- *Gary*: the entire session is questions ("What is life about?" / "How have you been living as if life is about what though?") until Gary supplies his own diagnosis unprompted.
- *Mike & Judy*: a full structured interview — 0–10 self-ratings across all six human needs, for both partners separately — run almost entirely as questions before any teaching occurs.
- Exception/counter-case: in *Scott*, after establishing needs via questions, Robbins does insert longer unprompted teaching monologues (the "salt/scotoma" story) — question-density drops noticeably once the core pattern is named, consistent with a "diagnose by question, then teach" rhythm rather than pure Socratic method throughout.

## 2. Challenge vs. support balance, and what triggers escalation to confrontation

Pattern across the corpus: **escalation is triggered by detected rationalization, hedging, or self-protective minimizing — not by the severity of the person's situation.** Raw, undefended grief gets gentleness; defended or intellectualized resistance gets confrontation, regardless of how "serious" the presenting problem is.
- Escalates hard: *Karen & Warren* — when Warren starts measuring his own emotional "centeredness" instead of feeling it: "No. If you have to ask, you weren't there... Don't be a Pussy." *Lindsey* — when she hedges/deflects about her husband: "When's the last time you blew him?"
- Stays gentle/spiritual: *Maggie* and *Gary* — both freshly bereaved, not rationalizing — get a slow, breath-paced register with no profanity directed at them ("Breathe deep... feel like you're filled with something that's always there," *Maggie*).
- What has to happen before he escalates: a visible gap between what the person says and what their body/behavior shows (Warren's hedging contradicts his own stated commitment; Lindsey's joking contradicts her stated anger) — the confrontation targets the *gap*, not the underlying pain itself.

## 3. Concretization — forcing vague statements into specific, measurable commitments

Signature move, present in nearly every file:
- **0–10 / 0–100 scaling of feelings**: "On a zero to ten, how certain are you...?" used on strangers within minutes of meeting them (*Mike & Judy*, *Paul & Jenn*, *Regina*, *Scott*, *Gary*, *Beverley*).
- **Mapping memories to specific fingers** one at a time rather than accepting "I don't have many good memories": "Give me the pinky one. The time you felt totally loved at a ten by him" (*Mike & Judy*) — and when Judy claims she can "count [good memories] on one hand," Robbins catches her undercounting mid-session and calls it a lie: "that thing of, 'I don't even have enough I could count on one hand.' That was one of those... tests."
- **Converting an abstract wish into a single sentence of action**: Scott's vague "I want to move out" is not accepted until he writes a literal list and makes a phone call live, on air (*Scott*).
- **Converting self-sabotage into a named, countable behavior**: Shelia's "I set myself up to be rejected" is drilled into an explicit cause-effect chain (*Shelia*) rather than left as a feeling.

## 4. Resistance handling — deflection, defensiveness, disagreement

- **Rejects "I don't know" as a real answer**: "Who is me?" / "I don't know." / "Yes, you do. Yes, you actually do. You just have been away from that part of you so long" (*Sukey*).
- **Forces a binary when a client hedges with "maybe"**: "Would you give me a yes or a no because good girls rarely say maybe?" (*Sukey*).
- **Reflects blame back onto the structure, not a side**, when clients try to make him referee: when the group debates who's "really" at fault in *Paul & Jenn*, he reframes repeatedly toward unmet needs rather than picking a guilty party.
- **Accepts a client's own metaphor/language and uses it as the tool rather than correcting it** — when the "bad girl" claims she'd "take over the world" if freed, Robbins doesn't argue the exaggeration is unrealistic; he works within it ("Look at this! Look at that big smile!") (*Sukey*) — Madanes's narration explicitly notes this as a deliberate respect for the client's own frame rather than correction.
- **When physically tested mid-session (a client deliberately tries to break his composure)**, he names the test out loud rather than reacting to it: Arontza drinks water mid-exercise to see if Alan will lose focus; Robbins narrates it live — "Don't lose Presence. What she's doing right now is testing you" (*Arontza & Alan*).

## 5. Reframing moves — breaking false binaries, shifting the frame

- **Vulnerability is power, not weakness** (*Lise*) — directly inverts her stated premise, using the Arthur Bremer story as proof.
- **Masculine and feminine aren't exclusive opposites a person must choose between — everyone has both, in imbalance** (*Lise*, *Mike & Judy*): "all four of these parts are inside of everybody in this room... they're not always in balance."
- **A warrior's purpose is not fighting but inner strength — the "mature warrior" reframe** (*Asad & Bernie*): "The purpose of the warrior is to find within you a greater level of inner strength, not to fight with others."
- **Integrity reframed from an externally imposed rule-set to "doing what you believe is right within yourself"** — and Robbins explicitly credits Beverley's own definition as superior to the common one (*Beverley*): "most people think integrity is a specific set of rules... but what you said is if they are doing what they believe is right within themselves, they have integrity. That's the highest and most accurate definition."
- **"Self-pity" reframed from an identity to a disposable intruder/vice to be destroyed** (*Regina*'s "Destroy the Vice" process) — explicitly not "manage your self-pity" but "I will never again indulge in... this emotion... it is not who I am."
- **Rejection reframed as self-sabotage with a payoff, not something done to you**: "I set it up... therefore I am significant, I achieved my goal" (*Shelia*).

## 6. Pacing & arc — how a session typically opens, builds, peaks, resolves

A consistent four-beat shape recurs across almost every file:
1. **Open**: light rapport/humor, often teasing, used to lower defenses before any real probing (*Karen & Warren*: "There's no hope for you" as an opener to a suicidal woman).
2. **Build**: question-driven mapping of the person's needs and "model of the world," usually the longest phase (*Sukey*, *Gary*, *Mike & Judy*).
3. **Peak**: a physiological/embodied marker — tears, a voice change, a posture shift, or a ritual physical action (breaking a chair — *Karen & Warren*; archetype-voicing — *Sukey*, *Shelia*; a live phone call — *Scott*, *Karen & Warren*). The peak is consistently non-verbal or ritualized, not a verbal "aha."
4. **Resolve**: a stated commitment, often repeated multiple times for intensity, followed by audience celebration (applause/cheers) functioning as a closing ritual in nearly every file.

## 7. Closing mechanism — what he insists on before ending

Robbins essentially never ends a session on insight alone; he insists on one of:
- **A literal spoken commitment/promise, often to a third party or an imagined presence**: Gary's new rule stated aloud to his daughter's imagined presence (*Gary*); Warren's "Chicago Forever" mantra, repeated multiple times until it lands with full conviction (*Mike & Judy*).
- **A concrete scheduled or immediate action**: Scott's live phone call to his mother (*Scott*); Lise's reordered assignment (sister before son) (*Lise*); Regina's repeated, escalating "Destroy the Vice" declaration, repeated until it's delivered with full physical intensity rather than accepted the first time it's said (*Regina*).
- **Extraction of a matching commitment from the other party, not just the client**: getting Scott's mother to commit on the phone that she won't let him move back in (*Scott*); getting both Karen and Warren to restate their commitment to honesty on camera (*Karen & Warren*).

## 8. Presence cues (only where the transcript itself notes them — nothing fabricated)

- `[Applause]`, `[Laughter]`, `[audience cheers]` — occur so frequently they function almost as punctuation across nearly every file.
- `{Alan pushes his hands downward to get centered}`, `{Alan kisses Arontza and looks in her eyes}`, `{Arontza finally shifts and holds Alan, her head on his shoulder}` — unusually detailed physical stage directions preserved in *Arontza & Alan* (this transcript's formatting is notably richer in stage direction than the others).
- `[Beverly throws off her sweater, audience cheers]`, `[Tony kisses Beverly on the cheek]` — physical escalation noted directly in *Beverley*.
- `[W crushes chair]` — the single most dramatic physical stage direction in the corpus (*Karen & Warren*).
- `[Tony inhales rapidly]` / `[Tony inhales and exhales deeply]` — the breathing exercise in *Maggie* has its own repeated, explicitly transcribed breath-sound notation, unique to that file.
- `[crying]`, `[whispered]`, `[Sukey laughs, audience cheers]` — vocal/emotional markers transcribed directly at multiple points across *Sukey*, *Lindsey*, *Lise*.

No instance was found in this group of a presence cue being asserted without in-text marking — all entries above are drawn directly from bracketed/braced annotations present in the source files.
